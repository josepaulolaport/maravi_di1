#### 🤖 Claude · 2026-10-18 21:33


Yes. The design already has everything needed to show, for a date, that its cash or its positions are being processed and how far the job has got. It cannot show the new figures before the job finishes; that is by design.

**What you already have**
- **Which date and which chain:** every job carries its range [A, B] and its scope (Caixa, Posições, Tudo). An active job (QUEUED, DISPATCHED, RUNNING, CANCELLING) whose range covers D and whose scope includes a chain means "that chain of D is being processed". So the screen can tell "the cash of 16/10 is being processed" apart from "the positions are", or both.
- **Where it is:** the job reports `processed_days`, `total_days` and `progress_date` (the day being computed now), because the worker goes one calendar day at a time. So "Processando: dia 3 de 5, em 16/10" is available, as is "Na fila" before it starts.
- **Updating live:** Central de Controle already polls the job list every ~2 s while any job is active, and stops when none is. When a job finishes, the app already refreshes the portfolio and job views (`invalidateAfterJobFinished`). The new figures then appear on their own.

**What it can't show**
- **Partial results while a job runs.** Publication is all or nothing (§10.4): days are staged, and nothing is visible until the whole range is published. Until then, Carteira and the cash strip keep showing the last published values (marked stale if they are). What changes live is the status, not the figures.
- **True push.** It is polling, not a server push. RabbitMQ only carries work to the worker and never reaches the browser, so "live" means about 2 seconds of delay.

**What it would take**
- **UI:** Carteira (for its date) and the Lançamentos cash strip (for D0 and D−1) don't read jobs today; only Central de Controle does. Each would get a small "Processando caixa · 2/5 dias" or "Na fila" marker next to the value. This is frontend-only work and uses the job list the screen can already fetch.
- **API (optional, for lane X):** today the only source is the full job list, which grows forever (OQ-5). A light read such as `GET /processing/active` (only active jobs), or a `processing` field on the portfolio summary (`{status, scope, progress_date}` per chain), would make it cheaper and avoid matching ranges in the browser.

If you want it, I can add that to the draft contract and the lane X proposals, and build the markers on Carteira and Lançamentos.

#### 🤖 Claude · 2026-10-18 21:37


## Where exactly the job is

Today the job reports progress to the **day**, not finer:

- `status`: Na fila, Enviado, Processando, Cancelando, then a final status;
- `progress_date`: the calendar day being computed right now;
- `processed_days` / `total_days`: days already computed and staged, e.g. "2 / 5 dias";
- `started_at`, and at the end `finished_at`.

The worker goes one day at a time, in order, and stages each finished day, so the day is the natural unit. It does not say *what* it is doing inside a day. If you want that, it is a small contract addition: a `phase` field, something like:
- "Calculando caixa"
- "Calculando posições"
- "Lendo preços"
- "Calculando P&L"
- "Publicando"

"Publicando" matters because the last step, the publish, is a real moment of its own: everything becomes visible at once. That would be a proposal to lane X; the worker can only report what it records.

## Why a job that stops early publishes none of its days

That came from two places:
- **Spec §10.4**, which already said each day is computed into a staging area per request and published in **one transaction** at the end. A cancelled or obsolete job discards its staging and earlier results stay as they were.
- **OQ-20 item 3.** The Aura HTML design for Processamentos showed "partial days" for a cancelled job. When you reviewed it, you asked me to explain how the atomic publish works, then chose "All or nothing (Recommended)".

The reasons behind that recommendation:

1. **Days are chained.** Each day's cash, positions and cumulative P&L are built from the day before. Publishing a mix (some days new, the following ones old) leaves the published series built on two different versions of the inputs.
2. **The inputs are checked at the end.** The job records the input revisions when it starts. At publish time it checks that nothing changed meanwhile, otherwise it is SUPERSEDED. Publishing day by day would expose days computed from inputs the user may have just changed.
3. **One consistent read.** Carteira reads cash, positions, position metrics and portfolio metrics together. A single transaction guarantees they always come from the same run.
4. **Cancel and recover simply.** A cancelled, failed or crashed job leaves nothing half-written: retry, or "Solicitar novo processamento" with the same range, starts clean.

**The cost is real:** a long range that fails or is cancelled near the end loses all its work.

**There is a sound middle ground** that was not chosen: publish the **completed prefix** [A, k] when a job stops. Days are only chained backwards, so A…k computed from A−1 is internally consistent. The rule would be:
- the input-revision check still applies before the prefix is published;
- markers move to k+1 (OQ-12);
- k+1…B simply stay stale.

It would change spec §10.4 and OQ-20 item 3, so it is your decision, recorded by lane X. If you want it, I can write it up as a proposal for lane X, or you can raise it there.

#### 🤖 Claude · 2026-10-18 21:42


I think it's a good change, and simpler than what we have. Each day is already self-contained, since it is built only from the day before, so committing days one at a time is sound. Two rules make it safe:
- **Stop at the first failure.** Later days depend on the day that failed.
- **Commit each day in one transaction**, with everything that day touches.

Here's the proposal, written for lane X:

---

## Proposal: process and publish day by day, without staging (changes §10.4 and OQ-20 item 3)

**Problem.** Today a job stages every day and publishes [A, B] in one transaction at the end. A job that fails, is cancelled or is superseded on its last day throws all the work away. While it runs, the user only sees "x / y dias", never which days are done.

**Proposal.** The worker processes [A, B] in calendar order. For each day D, in one transaction:
1. Check the job's claim token and lease, and its cancellation flag.
2. Compute D for the job's scope: the cash chain, the positions chain, or both for Tudo.
3. Upsert D's published rows: `cash_balances` and/or `positions`, `position_metrics` and `portfolio_metrics`. Recompute D's `aum` from the other chain's published value.
4. Move that chain's dirty marker past D: a marker on D or earlier inside [A, D] becomes D+1 (OQ-12, applied day by day).
5. Record D's status for this job.

The `staged_*` tables are no longer needed.

**Per-day status.** New table `processing.request_days(request_id, day, status, error_code, error_message, started_at, finished_at)`, one row per day of the range. Statuses:
- `PENDING` Na fila
- `RUNNING` Processando
- `DONE` Concluído
- `DONE_WITH_ISSUES` Concluído com pendências (e.g. a missing price)
- `FAILED` Erro (with the error)
- `NOT_RUN` Não executado (after a stop)

The UI shows exactly where the job is: which days are published, the day being computed, and where and why it stopped.

**Stop rules.** Later days depend on earlier ones, so the job never skips a day:
- **Technical error on D:** D is `FAILED`; D+1…B are `NOT_RUN`; the job ends `FAILED`. A…D−1 stay published.
- **Cancellation:** checked between days. The current day finishes and commits; the rest are `NOT_RUN`; the job ends `CANCELLED`.
- **A write during the run** dated W, on a chain in the job's scope:
  - W after the current day: the job continues. It reads inputs fresh for each day, so it picks the change up.
  - W on or before the current day: the job stops before the next day (`SUPERSEDED`, or a new name such as "Interrompido"). Days ≥ W that were already published become stale through the normal marker. That is correct: they read Desatualizado until reprocessed.
- **Missing price:** not an error. The day is `DONE_WITH_ISSUES` and the job continues, as today.
- **Missing cash predecessor (OQ-22):** refused at start as today, so no day runs.

**Crash and lease recovery.** A new worker resumes from the first day not marked `DONE`, instead of restarting the range.

**What a reader sees mid-run.** Each day is consistent with its predecessor. A day not yet reprocessed keeps its previous result and its own freshness flag, so Carteira for that day reads Desatualizado or Não processado, never a broken mix.

**What we give up:**
- The range is no longer published as one unit. A cancelled or failed job leaves its first days published; OQ-20 item 3 said it publishes nothing.
- There is one transaction per day instead of one per range. This is fine at our scale.
- The input check moves from "nothing changed since start" to "nothing changed on or before the day being computed". The markers cover the rest.

**Docs to change** (lane X; this needs your decision, recorded as a new open question):
- spec §10.2 and §10.4;
- OQ-20 items 3 and 4: "Solicitar novo processamento" should prefill from the first day not done, not the full range;
- OQ-5 and OQ-8: staging lifecycle and staging tables;
- `interfaces.md`: `PortfolioResultsWriter.stage_day` / `publish_range` become one `write_day`, plus the publish steps;
- `schema.sql` and a migration: drop the `staged_*` tables, including the staging twin of `cash_balances` from 0002, and add `request_days`;
- plan §6.

**Frontend:**
- the details modal lists the days with their statuses;
- the history row shows "3 / 5 dias · erro em 16/10";
- the Carteira status can show "Processando" or "Erro" for the exact day;
- the draft contract adds `ProcessingRequest.days[]` or `GET /processing/requests/{id}/days`.

---

**My recommendation:** do it, with exactly those rules. My one concern is timing. Delivery is Monday, and this touches the Processing and Portfolio lanes and migration 0002, which is still in review. If lane X thinks it's too late, a fallback is to keep the staging and publish the completed prefix [A, k] when a job stops. That gets most of the benefit and changes less.
